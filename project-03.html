CASE STUDY 01

User research for a hair care app

Turning a complex user problem into a validated digital product concept.

The project explored how a digital product could better support the curly-haired community with hair-care decisions. Rather than beginning with a predefined solution, I used a Design Thinking process to investigate the problem space, involve users throughout, and continuously turn research findings into product decisions.

#01 [THE CHALLENGE]
From information overload to meaningful guidance.

Hair-care information is abundant, but abundance does not necessarily make decision-making easier. The challenge was to understand what people actually struggle with, where existing digital experiences fall short, and what kind of support would feel genuinely useful to the curly-haired community.

The goal was therefore not simply to design another hair-care app. It was to discover which problems were worth solving first, and to let the users themselves influence the direction of the product.

#02 [RESEARCH APPROACH]
Research before solutions.

I used multiple research methods to look at the problem from different perspectives. Six semi-structured interviews helped uncover individual behaviours, pain points and needs, while a survey added quantitative evidence to the qualitative findings.

The process then moved from understanding → defining → ideating → validating, iterating between stages rather than treating Design Thinking as a linear process.

#03 [FROM RESEARCH TO INSIGHT]
Connecting the dots.

The interviews gave me depth; the survey strengthened the patterns emerging from them. I then mapped the relationship between users and the product through a User Journey, identifying actions, pain points and emotional states across the experience. These findings became the basis for How Might We questions, shifting the project from describing problems towards exploring opportunities.

This was an important transition: I was no longer designing based on assumptions, I was designing around evidence.

#04 [TURNING RESEARCH INTO DIRECTION]
Turning research into direction.

During the Define phase I used a SWOT analysis to step back from the research findings and assess the concept as a whole, its strengths, its weaknesses, the opportunities it could realistically pursue and the threats it would face.

It kept me from moving straight from research into solution-building. Seeing the findings in that structure made the design decisions that followed more deliberate, I could weigh what the concept was well positioned to do against what it was not.

#05 [FROM USER INPUT TO PRODUCT SCOPE]
Turning feedback into product decisions.

Three priorities kept recurring across the research, Products · Personalization · Community, and they became the pillars for defining the product scope.

Each proposed feature was then evaluated against these priorities, turning a broad collection of ideas into a focused product concept: research findings → priorities → features.

PRODUCT INFORMATION

Helping users understand and evaluate hair-care products by making relevant product information easier to access and compare.

PERSONALIZATION

Tailoring the experience to each user’s hair needs by turning their individual characteristics and preferences into relevant routines and recommendations.

COMMUNITY

Creating a space where users can learn from people with similar hair, discover relevant content, and exchange experiences. The current community space is scattered, and I want to bring that scattered attention together in one app.

#06 [CO-CREATING THE PRODUCT]
Users became part of the design process.

Instead of treating research participants as people I only interviewed, I involved them again when the concept started taking shape. I facilitated a focus group with six participants from the target audience, structured around concept discussion, prioritisation and idea generation.

Participants evaluated potential functionality and contributed their own ideas for the product, allowing me to challenge my assumptions before committing to a direction. They also received ranking and prioritization tasks that finalized the validation and decision making.

#07 [FIT IN THE MARKET]
Fit in the market.

A Lean Canvas let me examine whether the concept could work as an early-stage product, not only whether it was desirable from a UX perspective. It connected the research findings, information overload, difficulty understanding hair types and the need for trustworthy personalised guidance, with the target users, the value proposition, the solution and a potential business model.

#08 [VALIDATING THE CONCEPT]
From sketches to something testable.

Once the product direction was defined, I translated the concept into sketches and an interactive prototype. I used Think-Aloud testing to observe how users interacted with the experience and identify where the concept needed adjustment.

The prototype was not treated as a final answer. It became another research tool, allowing me to test assumptions, observe behaviour and refine the experience.

#09 [OUTCOME]
A concept shaped by its users.

The result was a digital concept designed around three connected needs: making product information easier to navigate, making recommendations more personal, and creating space for the already strong and communicative community.

More importantly, the final direction was not decided in isolation. Interviews, survey responses, co-creation and prototype testing continuously influenced the product throughout the process.

#10 [WHAT THIS PROJECT SHOWS]

Research with purpose

I learned to choose research methods based on the question I needed to answer, combining qualitative and quantitative approaches instead of relying on a single source of information.

Turning evidence into decisions

The project strengthened my ability to synthesise research, identify patterns and translate them into concrete product priorities and design opportunities.

Working with users as stakeholders

By involving users not only as research participants but also in concept evaluation, prioritisation and ideation, I gained experience facilitating collaboration and incorporating different perspectives into product decisions.

Validating before committing

Testing the prototype allowed me to challenge assumptions early and iterate based on observed behaviour and feedback.

FINAL TAKEAWAY

Design with people, not just for them.

This project strengthened my ability to move between research, synthesis, facilitation and design, using user input to shape decisions rather than simply validating decisions I had already made.
